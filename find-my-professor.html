Step by step

How to email a professor for a funded master's

Professors get many emails. A short, specific one gets read.

01

Shortlist 8–15 professors

Use the filters above. Pick people whose recent topics match what you want to research, not just your degree title.

02

Read their recent work

Open 2–3 of their recent papers. Note one specific idea you found interesting and how your skills could help.

03

Check the program first

Look at the department's master's admission page: deadlines, minimum grades, English test scores, and whether you apply first or contact a supervisor first.

04

Write 150–200 words

Subject: "Prospective MSc student – [topic]". Say who you are, which of their papers interested you and why, your relevant skills, and ask if they're taking students for [term]. Attach your CV and transcript.

05

Use their official email

Click "Official profile" on the card to find their current email on the university website. Send one personal email per professor, not a mass email.

06

Follow up once

No reply after 10–14 days? One polite follow-up is fine. Then move on; many professors simply don't have space that year.

Funding for research master's in Canada usually comes from the supervisor's grants, university scholarships, or teaching/research assistantships. Ask the professor and the department what's available.
