Canada's new immigration plan: what it means for students

Meskhat HossainNovember 12, 2026 · 6 min read

In November 2026, IRCC published the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan. Every year Canada sets targets for how many temporary and permanent residents it will admit over the next three years. If you're planning to study in Canada, here's what this plan says and why it matters to you.

The key numbers

TargetPer year, 2027–2029
New temporary resident arrivals370,000
…of which new international studentsup to 150,000
…of which new workers220,000
Permanent resident admissions380,000

IRCC says permanent resident admissions in the economic class will stay above 64% of the total, and that these admissions include people who are already working or studying in Canada, not only new arrivals.

What this means for you

  1. Study spots remain limited. With a cap of up to 150,000 new students a year, a strong, well-prepared application matters more than ever.
  2. Choose your program carefully. Pick a program that makes sense for your background and career plan, and explain that clearly in your study plan.
  3. Think long term. Because PR admissions include people already studying and working in Canada, what you do after graduation (your work experience) is a big part of the path to permanent residence.

The Express Entry change (November 3, 2026)

Separately, on October 16, 2026, IRCC announced changes to the Express Entry Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS). Starting November 3, 2026, IRCC awards additional points to candidates with eligible Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation, such as engineers and electricians.

For students, the message is clear: Canadian work experience after graduation counts for even more. Planning your career early, from your program choice to your first job, can make a real difference later.
