Money

How much money do you need to study in Canada?

Meskhat HossainNovember 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Proof of funds is one of the biggest parts of a Canadian study permit application, and one of the most common reasons students from Bangladesh get worried. Here's what IRCC actually asks for right now, in plain language.

What you have to prove

According to IRCC, you must show you have enough money, without working in Canada, to pay for all of the following:

  • tuition fees for your program
  • living expenses for you and any family members who come with you
  • transportation to and from Canada for you and your family members

You need to show enough for the first year of your studies. If your program is longer than a year, you must also explain how you'll pay for the full length of it, for example with a multi-year scholarship or proof that your parents are employed.

The new living-cost amounts

IRCC updates these amounts every year. If you apply on or after September 1, 2026, these are the minimum living-cost amounts for one year, for every province and territory except Quebec:

People coming (including you)Amount per year (CAN$)
1 (just you)$23,448
2$29,192
3$35,888
4$43,572
Important: these amounts do not include tuition or travel. Those come on top. So a single student must show $23,448 plus first-year tuition plus travel costs.

Studying in Quebec is different: you'll need a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) and must meet Quebec's own financial requirements.

Don't forget the PAL/TAL

In most cases you also need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL/TAL) from your school. IRCC notes that you usually have to accept your offer and pay some or all of your tuition to get one, so plan that payment into your budget early.

Practical tips for students from Bangladesh

  1. Budget in Canadian dollars first, then convert using the current exchange rate on the day you plan, and leave a buffer, because rates move.
  2. Make the source of your money clear. IRCC looks at how much money you have and where it comes from, so keep your documents consistent and easy to follow.
  3. Show a plan for later years if your program is longer than one year.
  4. Remember real life costs more than the minimum. The IRCC figure is what you must show, not necessarily what you'll spend, especially in big cities.
